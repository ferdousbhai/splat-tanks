# Splat Tanks audit (2026-10-10)

This audit uses the same standard as the twelve BitGames originals (bitgames `docs/polish/AUDIT_2026-10-09.md`). Games should be calm and gentle and build sustained focus, with learning inside the play, and avoid attention hooks. The owner's children count this among their favourites, so what they love stays: driving a toy tank, squirting paint, growing from Squirter to Titan, and playing with friends on other devices.

Evidence: a solo 3-minute match at 1194×834 in Chromium (screenshots in the session scratchpad `splat/audit/`) and a full read of `public/js`.

## Attention hooks found

- **Clock pressure:** the timer turns red and pulses at 30 s, with a "30 SECONDS LEFT!" banner and a spoken warning (`hud.js` `timer`, `main.js` `battleStep`).
- **Hit-marker loop:** "SPLAT! 🎨" crit floaters, a hit-marker ping and a crosshair flash every time you splash a tank, plus 12 XP for doing it (`main.js` `onHit`, `splashTank`). This rewards aiming at friends over painting.
- **Reward spam:** a "LEVEL N!" banner with a 5-note arpeggio about every 15 s, confetti on every evolve and every bucket, and 26 confetti cubes per tank at the end.
- **Screen and sound:** camera shake on buckets; bouncy 120 bpm music with hi-hats and a square-wave lead; shouted ALL-CAPS pickup text ("SUPER SQUIRT!"); pickups that bob and pulse quickly.
- **Speech:** every line cancels the previous one, at pitch 1.3.
- **Ranking:** a live leaderboard, a 👑 over the leader, and 🥇🥈🥉 medals that robots usually win.
- **Interruptions:** upgrade cards stay on screen until picked, often while the child is mid-drive.

## Smaller issues

- `hud.toast` hides on an untracked timer, so a second toast vanishes early.
- There is no way back to the menu after a match except reloading, so the host can't change robots or time.
- With reduced motion turned on, the banners still spring and the level badge still bumps.

## Plan

1. **Calm pass for every mode.** Soft countdown with no shouted GO; a steady clock with no warning; no shake; no crit floaters, hit-marker or crosshair flash; confetti only for growing up a tier; gentler music (no hi-hats or square lead); sentence-case pickups; slower bobbing; the shared queued voice; `prefers-reduced-motion`; a 🏠 for the host in play and on the results screen.
2. **Learning in the core loop: Paint together, the new default.** The floor is split into equal parts with chalk lines: halves, thirds or quarters. Everyone paints together with no clock, and the robots help. A fraction strip shows each part filling up. When a part is nearly painted, its gaps fill in and the game names the fraction: "One quarter painted! Three quarters to go." Two quarters is named as one half. The round ends at "Four quarters make one whole!". Every tank grows a tier each time a part is finished, so growing becomes the reward for finishing a part instead of grinding XP, and there are no upgrade cards to interrupt. Quarters alternate between squares and strips: different shapes, still quarters.
3. **The splat match stays** as a menu choice ⏱️, with the calm pass applied. Its levels, upgrade cards and per-player paint shares remain, since that is the game the children know.

No Blender work is needed. The chalk lines and fraction labels are simple runtime geometry, as the asset rules ask.
